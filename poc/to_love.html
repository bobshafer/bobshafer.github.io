Restatement of your request:

You are asking for a precise definition of the verb “to love,” formulated strictly within the Principle of Continuation (PoC) framework — i.e., in terms of continuation categories, viability, elastic/plastic regimes, record, and compatibility — rather than in psychological or poetic language.

Now I will answer that.

1. The PoC primitives we must use

From the current formal structure:

  • Objects: states (agents, systems, configurations).
  • Morphisms: continuations.
  • Viability \(v(\gamma)\): multiplicative under serial composition.
  • Elastic regime: plural possible continuations.
  • Commitment regime: irreversible record.
  • History: accumulated committed morphisms.
  • Lawvere metric: cost \(D(\gamma) = -\ln v(\gamma)\).

We must define “to love” using only these structures.

2. First elimination: what love cannot mean in PoC

Love cannot simply mean:

  • High immediate viability.
  • Maximal local reward.
  • Shortest-cost path.

Those correspond to optimization, preference, or selection — but not love.

Love must involve:

  • Persistence across time.
  • Cost tolerance.
  • Structural coupling of continuation spaces.
  • Record-level commitment, not just elastic preference.

So love must be a relation between continuation structures, not a single transition.

3. Minimal PoC definition (first pass)

Let A and B be objects (agents/systems) in \(\mathbf C\).

We say:

A loves B if A’s continuation structure is reweighted such that continuations preserving or enhancing B’s viability are incorporated into A’s own viable future set.

Formally:

Love is a modification of A’s viability measure:

\[v_A(\gamma) \longrightarrow v_A^{(B)}(\gamma)\]

such that morphisms supporting B’s continuation acquire increased viability weight within A’s continuation geometry.

This is already nontrivial:

Love alters the geometry of A’s sphere of becoming.

4. Stronger definition (structural coupling)

The minimal version above could still describe strategic alliance.

Love must go deeper: it must involve coupling of histories.

Define joint continuation:

\[\mathbf C_{A\otimes B}\]

Love corresponds to formation of a tensor-like continuation coupling in which:

  1. A’s future viability depends on B’s viability.
  2. A incurs nonlocal cost penalties when B’s continuation degrades.
  3. Record maps become partially shared (intertwined histories).

Formally:

There exists a non-separable continuation state \[\Psi_{AB} \not= \psi_A \otimes \psi_B\]

such that A’s and B’s viability metrics are no longer independent.

Love is entanglement at the continuation level.

5. Elastic vs committed love

Elastic affection (O-mode):

  • Many possible joint futures.
  • Superposition of shared possibilities.
  • Exploration.

Committed love (G-mode):

  • Record-level identification.
  • Irreversible incorporation of B into A’s history.
  • Viability of A now functionally includes B.

The transition O → G in love is precisely commitment.

Love that remains purely elastic is attraction. Love that crosses into record is binding.

6. Cost tolerance as diagnostic

Because viability multiplies under serial composition, love must permit continuation even when:

\[ v_A(\gamma) \text{ is low for A alone} \]

but

\[ v_{A\otimes B}(\gamma) \text{ is high jointly} \]

Thus love permits acceptance of local cost increase for global continuation preservation.

In Lawvere metric terms:

Love allows A to accept longer geodesics in its own metric if they reduce joint distance in the coupled metric.

That is not preference; it is metric restructuring.

7. Formal definition (clean version)

Let \(A,B\in\mathbf C\).

A loves B iff:

  1. There exists a continuation coupling functor

    \[ F: \mathbf C_A \times \mathbf C_B \to \mathbf C_{AB} \]

    producing non-separable joint states.

  2. A’s viability functional is reweighted such that

    \[ v_A(\gamma_A) = \sum_{\gamma_B} w(\gamma_B)\, v_{AB}(\gamma_A,\gamma_B) \]

    with positive dependence on B’s continuation.

  3. The record map of A incorporates events concerning B into A’s Boolean event algebra.

In simpler language:

Love is the irreversible incorporation of another’s continuation into one’s own viability geometry.

8. What love is not in PoC

  • Not mere attraction (elastic bias without commitment).
  • Not dependency (one-sided viability collapse).
  • Not optimization (maximizing own viability).
  • Not fusion (identity collapse).

Love preserves distinct objects but couples their continuation metrics.

9. The deepest compression

In PoC:

Self = stabilized continuation pattern. Love = expansion of the self’s continuation boundary to include another stabilized pattern as co-essential.

So the verb “to love” means:

To expand one’s sphere of becoming so that another’s persistence becomes structurally necessary to one’s own.

That is precise in PoC language.

10. Even more compressed

Love is non-separable continuation with shared record.
